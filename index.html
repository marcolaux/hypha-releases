Where I run

A notes app with nothing behind it. No account, no server, no company holding your words. Your devices keep the vault, encrypt it, and sync it with each other — and with no one else.

Fig. 0.1

Local-first

Your notes live in a database on your device. Opening, searching and writing never wait for a network, because there is no network in the way.

Fig. 0.2

No one in the middle

There is no account to create and no server to trust. Your devices find each other and talk directly — on your Wi-Fi, or across the internet. I collect nothing.

Fig. 0.3

One passphrase

Every note is encrypted before it leaves this device, with a key that comes from your passphrase. That passphrase is the only credential there is. Keep it.

Write

I'm where you write.

Notes in notebooks, with tags and bookmarks. Headings, lists that fold, tasks, tables that calculate, callouts, code, drawings, pictures and files — all in one page, and every page styled the way you like.

In detail
  • +Type @ to link a note, or # to tag it — the chip in the text is the relation, not a copy of it. Link to a note that does not exist yet and I write it for you.
  • +Tables calculate: =SUM(B2:B9) shows its result, and German numbers work. Type 3 + 3 = and the answer appears.
  • +Paste Markdown and it becomes structure. A whiteboard is a block like any other, and so is a PDF you can highlight.
  • +Page styles: paper, textures, fonts and a cover picture per note, or one look for the whole vault. Every note keeps its history, so you can restore what you wrote last Tuesday.

Organise

A note can be a recipe, a book, a project — or anything you define.

Give a note a type and it carries properties under its title: a rating, a date, a status, the people involved, a link to another note. Then look at any notebook, tag, bookmark or type as cards, a list, a table, a board or a calendar.

In detail
  • +Movie, TV series, Book, Recipe, Article, Product, Event, Project, Meeting, Contact — or a type of your own, with text, numbers, ratings, dates, durations, choices, checkboxes, lists, and links to notes and people.
  • +Saved views on every page: a table with a column per property you can edit in place, a board in columns made from any property, a calendar by any date. They sync; the phone shows them as chips.
  • +Filters that read like sentences: rating at least four, edited in the last week, tagged any of these. Rollups and formulas sum up what a project's notes hold.
  • +A bookmark is a colour that marks a whole note and the passages you highlight — in a note or in a PDF. Its page lists every one of them.

Days

I keep your days.

Every day gets its own note, in one continuous stream — with the day's tasks, the events of your calendar, and the weather, if you let me.

In detail
  • +Scroll back through your days like one long page. The day you are on stays pinned at the top, and a rail beside it marks what happened when.
  • +The day's tasks sit under the day's writing — including tasks you gave a date from any other note. Reminders fire on every device, and a notice stays at the top of today until it is done.
  • +Subscribe to a calendar — an .ics address or a Nextcloud sign-in — and its events become bands on the day. One tap writes a Meeting note for an event.
  • +"On this day" shows what you wrote a year ago. Weather is optional and uses a coarse location. On a Mac or iPhone with Apple Intelligence, one tap summarises the day.

Find

I find what you meant.

Search every note in full, or by what you meant: ask for bread that came out flat and I find the rye loaf that never says so. Then look at the whole vault as a map, grouped by what the notes are about.

In detail
  • +Full-text search reads the note, not just the title, and finds other forms of a word. Search by meaning works too, over embeddings a model inside the app computes on your computer — the words never leave your devices.
  • +Text in your pictures and words in your recordings are read on the device and searchable too. So are chat messages and comments.
  • +The Note map places every note by meaning, finds the groups and names them; a second view draws the links between notes. Every note shows what points at it.
  • +Your phone searches the index your computer built, and embeds only the question — natively, on the phone. A vault that lives on a phone alone has full-text search.

Files

Everything that belongs with a note, in the note.

Photos, scans, voice notes, PDFs and files are stored encrypted and de-duplicated, fetched on demand by the devices that want them. A drawing is a block in the page; a PDF opens in it, and a highlight in the PDF is something you can link to.

In detail
  • +Draw on a whiteboard inside a note with a pen, shapes and text; two devices drawing at once merge. Handwriting becomes searchable text on the newest systems.
  • +Highlight a passage in a PDF, give it a bookmark's colour, and paste a reference to it into any note — the reference opens the PDF at that passage.
  • +A picture's text and a recording's words are read on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere to make it so.

Together

Share a vault with the people who share your life.

A vault is not a login; it is a thing your devices hold. Add another device — yours or someone else's — and it holds the vault too. Then talk about the notes right next to them.

A family

The shopping list, the trip, the renovation, the kids' schedule: one vault on every phone in the house, a chat beside it, and no company reading any of it.

A few colleagues

Meeting notes, a shared reading list, the running document for a project. Your notes stay on your laptops, not on a server your employer rents.

Just you

A Mac, an iPhone, the old laptop at your parents' place. Write on any of them; the others catch up the next time they meet.

One chat per vault — replies, reactions, pins, pictures — and comments anchored to a passage of a note that follow the text as it changes. Everything sealed and signed like a note.

Sync

I sync between your devices — and only there.

I encrypt every note before it leaves this device. Your devices talk to each other directly, over your Wi-Fi or across the internet, and no server ever reads a word. The keys live on your devices and nowhere else.

In detail
  • +On the same network, devices find each other by themselves. Across the internet, they meet through a distributed hash table and connect end to end.
  • +Edits made in two places at once merge without conflicts. Each device holds the whole vault, so any one of them can regrow the rest.
  • +An optional relay carries encrypted updates for devices that are rarely online together. It never holds a key. Run your own, or ask for an invite code to hypha's relay while it is in beta.
  • +Several vaults, each with its own passphrase, devices and look. Touch ID or Face ID to unlock. A Mac keeps syncing from the menu bar with no window open.

Always on

A filament that is always there.

Two devices that are never online at the same time still need a way to meet. hypha-peer is that way: a small program that stores encrypted updates and hands them on, and cannot read any of them.

$ curl -LO https://github.com/marcolaux/hypha-releases/releases/latest/download/hypha-peer-compose.yml
$ curl -LO https://github.com/marcolaux/hypha-releases/releases/latest/download/hypha-peer.env.example
$ mv hypha-peer-compose.yml docker-compose.yml && mv hypha-peer.env.example .env
# set HYPHA_PEER_SECRETS_PASSPHRASE in .env — openssl rand -base64 32
$ docker compose run --rm peer init
$ docker compose run --rm peer join "hypha://invite/…"
$ docker compose up -d

The compose file and the env example ship with every release; the install guide has the rest.

In detail
  • +Runs anywhere you can run Docker or systemd — a home server, a NAS, a Raspberry Pi, a rented box. Linux is the right home.
  • +It joins a vault as a Relay, from an invite like any other device. On your own network it can offer itself, and your devices ask you once.
  • +It never holds a vault key. If someone takes the box, they have ciphertext.

And

The rest of what I do.

  • Web clipper

    Clip a page from the browser or the share sheet. A recipe arrives as a Recipe, a film as a Movie, with its details filled in from the page — cleaned on the way in.

  • Quick capture

    A global shortcut on the Mac opens a small window; the note lands in today's note. On iPhone, the Action button, a Control or Siri do the same.

  • Templates

    A template is a note that others start from, with the date filled in. A note type can name its own.

  • Panes and windows

    Tabs, a split editor, a tab torn off into its own window. Every window is the same vault, and they are put back the way you left them.

  • Publish a note

    Publish a single note as a static page to a bucket you own — optionally encrypted, so the bucket holds only ciphertext.

  • Lock

    Touch ID on the Mac, Face ID on the iPhone, auto-lock when idle, and Lock vault now from the command palette.

  • Themes

    Light and dark, following the system or not; dozens of themes, and your own as a file.

  • On iPhone and iPad

    Lock Screen widgets, the share sheet, voice notes with transcription, a document scanner. The iPad gets the desktop layout.

  • Version history

    Every note keeps its history, whiteboards included. Restore what you wrote last Tuesday, or just look.

  • Archive and trash

    Put a note away without deleting it. The trash keeps what you delete for a while, and a restore merges — it never clobbers newer notes.

  • English and German

    The whole app in both, with date, number and sentence conventions to match.

  • Made to be read

    Text size per device, every phone control sized for a thumb, a sidebar that screen readers can walk, and reduced motion honoured.

Questions

Asked before.

Do I need an account?
No. There is no account, no e-mail address and no server to sign up with. You choose a passphrase, and a vault lives on your devices. A second device joins through an invite from the first — a QR code or a link.
Where are my notes stored?
On each of your devices, in an encrypted database. Nothing sits on a server unless you add a relay, and a relay holds only ciphertext it cannot read.
Can anyone else read my notes?
No. Every note is encrypted on the device with keys derived from your passphrase, before it travels anywhere. The peer network that introduces your devices and any relay in between see ciphertext. The cryptography has not been audited yet; the known limitations say so.
How do my devices find each other?
On the same Wi-Fi, by themselves. Across the internet, through a distributed hash table, after which they connect end to end. Devices that are never online together meet through an optional relay — one you run, or hypha's while it is in beta.
What if I lose a device, or forget the passphrase?
Every device holds the whole vault, so a lost device costs you no notes: revoke it from any other, and restore a new one from any other or from a backup, which hypha keeps for you by day, week and month. A forgotten passphrase cannot be reset by anyone — there is no account to reset it through. Keep it somewhere safe.
Which platforms?
macOS on Apple silicon, Windows, Linux, and iPhone and iPad through TestFlight while the beta lasts. Intel Macs are not supported. The iPad gets the desktop layout.
What does it cost, and is it open source?
The apps cost nothing. hypha is source-available, not open source: you may use it for anything, read it, change it and share it, at no charge — you may not sell it. hypha's own relay is in beta, by invite code.
Can I bring my notes, and take them with me?
Yes. Import an Obsidian vault, any folder of Markdown or a Standard Notes backup. Export a whole vault as Markdown, a note as PDF, or a portable backup that restores on a fresh device.

Where I stand

Beta. Honest about it.

The apps are real and their author uses them every day, on a Mac and an iPhone. There are things you should know before you trust me with anything you would hate to lose — the cryptography has not been audited yet, Windows and Linux are tested only occasionally, and a few paths have not been walked on a phone by anyone. They are all in one list, kept honest and current.
